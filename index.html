01 — UX Strategy

A layered approach
to conversational support

The strategy centred on reducing human agent load for Depend's customer service while maintaining the empathy and trust that the brand's audience — often navigating sensitive health needs — requires.

01
Self-Service First

Design a guided flow that answers the most common queries autonomously — product selection, sample requests, coupons, and FAQs — before escalating. This reduces agent queue volume and provides immediate resolution for users.

Guided menus Quick replies Topic taxonomy
02
Graceful Escalation

When the bot cannot resolve an issue, users can invoke "Talk to an agent" at any point. The handoff preserves conversation context — the human agent sees exactly where the user was in the flow, eliminating repetition.

Context handoff Always-on escape Live agent bridge
03
Brand-Voice Alignment

Val's persona was crafted to reflect Depend's warm, supportive brand tone. From the greeting copy to the button labels, every piece of language was reviewed for empathy, clarity, and accessibility.

Tone of voice Accessibility Brand fidelity
04
Information Architecture

Five top-level intents were identified through support ticket analysis: Products & Samples, Coupons, Advice/Support/FAQs, Registration issues, and direct agent access. Sub-trees were designed to be no deeper than 2 levels.

5 intents ≤2 levels deep Sub-topics
02 — Research & Insights

What the data
told us to build

Insights were drawn from customer support logs, usability benchmarks for conversational interfaces, and accessibility audits for users 50+.

🗂️
Button-Based Navigation

Free-text input created high rates of unrecognised intent. Switching to structured quick-reply buttons reduced misrouted queries significantly and improved task completion for users unfamiliar with chatbots.

🎯
Top 3 Intents = 68% of Volume

Products & Samples, Coupons, and Advice/FAQs together accounted for the majority of incoming support requests — guiding the priority order of menu items and sub-flows.

♿
Accessibility-First Design

Depend's core demographic skews 50+. High-contrast colour pairings, generous tap targets (minimum 44px), Mulish typeface at legible sizes, and clear visual hierarchy were all informed by this user profile.

⚡
Immediate Routing Reduces Friction

Users who saw categorised options within the first bot message showed higher engagement than those presented with open prompts. The welcome message with immediate options was a key UX decision.

🤝
Agent Handoff Expectation

Research showed users feel frustrated if they can't reach a human — even if they don't use it. Exposing "Talk to an agent" as a persistent top-level option increased overall trust and completion rates.

🔁
Context Must Carry Forward

A key pain point in existing flows was that escalation to a live agent reset the conversation. The handoff design ensures the agent receives a transcript of the bot interaction to eliminate redundant questioning.

03 — Problem & Solution

The challenge and
how we resolved it

Depend's existing support model relied heavily on live agents for queries that were repetitive, predictable, and fully self-serviceable.

⚠ The Problem
A support system not built for scale or sensitivity
  • High agent workload from repetitive, low-complexity queries (samples, coupons, store locator)
  • No digital self-service channel — customers had to call or email for routine requests
  • Chatbot prototypes used open text input, causing frequent intent mismatches and user drop-off
  • When users were transferred to agents, context was lost — leading to frustrating repeat explanations
  • Brand tone was absent from digital touch-points, creating a disconnect from Depend's empathetic identity
✦ The Solution
A structured, empathetic chatbot with seamless agent bridging
  • Designed a structured quick-reply taxonomy mapping the 5 highest-volume support intents into navigable menus
  • Introduced "Val", a named virtual agent with a warm, on-brand persona crafted in collaboration with the copy team
  • Replaced free-text fields with categorised button flows to reduce unrecognised intent to near-zero
  • Built a context-preserving agent handoff — the full chat transcript travels with the escalation to the live agent
  • Colour system and layout meet WCAG AA contrast requirements, optimised for users 50+

01 — UX Strategy

Evaluating a live tool
to build a better one

The strategic goal was to evaluate the existing Kotex Period Calculator against real user expectations — identifying friction, feature gaps, and opportunities to evolve the tool into a more accurate and empowering digital health experience.

01
Understand Current Behaviour

Rather than building from assumptions, the research began with how users already track periods. Understanding existing workflows — apps, calendars, notes, Fitbit — revealed what any new or improved tool would need to compete with and complement.

Behaviour audit Habit mapping App benchmarking
02
Validate the Existing Tool

Contributors tested the live NA Period Calculator on the UserTesting platform. Post-test questions captured candid reactions to usability, design, content, and feature expectations — forming the backbone of recommendations.

03
Identify Feature Gaps

The research surfaced a consistent gap between what the tool offered and what users needed: calendar sync, editable cycle dates, symptom tracking, and personalised predictions were all absent but strongly desired.

02 — Research & Insights

What 12 contributors
told us to change

Insights drawn from moderated usability sessions, post-test questionnaires, and qualitative analysis of contributor comments across the NA Period Calculator experience.

📱
Mobile App Is the Gold Standard

7 of 12 contributors use dedicated mobile apps to track periods. The convenience of phone notifications and in-app insights set a high bar — any web-based tool must justify why users shouldn't just open Clue or Flo instead.

🗓️
Calendar Sync Is a Dealbreaker

The top-requested feature was the ability to sync period and ovulation dates directly to Google, Outlook, or Apple Calendar. Users want their health data where they already manage their lives — not in a separate system.

✏️
Editability Builds Trust

Contributors want to edit predicted period dates, adjust cycle lengths, and correct entries. A read-only tool feels inaccurate and powerless — editability is core to the perception of accuracy and personal relevance.

🩺
Symptom Tracking is Expected

Users expect to log mood, pain, flow intensity, and other symptoms alongside dates. Several contributors noted this was their primary reason for using dedicated apps over calendars — the Period Calculator was missing this entirely.

🎨
Design Was Well-Received

Contributors praised the clean, minimal visual design and the distinct colour coding for period vs ovulation days. The FAQs, Tips & Advice section, and the downloadable calendar were highlighted as standout positives.

📧
Email Reminders Need Control

Users wanted the ability to control the frequency of email reminders and to receive either reminders or educational resources — but not both combined. Being unable to separate these was a frustration point across multiple contributors.

03 — Problem & Solution

The gap between
what exists and what's needed

The current NA Period Calculator performed well on usability and aesthetics — but fell short of the feature depth users expect from a digital health tool in 2023.

⚠ Current State — Gaps Found
A useful tool with significant feature limitations
  • No calendar sync — users cannot export results to Google, Outlook, or Apple Calendar
  • Period dates are not editable after generation — reduces trust for users with irregular cycles
  • No symptom tracking — mood, pain, and flow cannot be logged, making the tool feel shallow vs. apps like Clue
  • Email reminders cannot be customised in frequency or content type (reminders vs. resources)
  • Tool shows only one month at a time — users want to see upcoming period and ovulation days across multiple months
  • No app version — the majority of contributors strongly prefer mobile, yet the tool is web-only
✦ Recommended Solutions
A redesigned experience that grows with the user
  • Add one-click calendar export to Google, Outlook, and Apple Calendar from the results screen
  • Enable editable cycle dates and adjustable period length in the calculator input form
  • Introduce a basic symptom log (mood, flow, pain) accessible from the calendar results view
  • Add an email preference centre — separate toggles for reminders vs. editorial content, with frequency controls
  • Extend the calendar output to display 3–6 months of upcoming periods and ovulation windows
  • Develop a progressive web app (PWA) or companion mobile app to serve the majority mobile-first audience
04 — Feature Recommendations

Prioritised feature
roadmap

Ten recommendations emerged from the research, ranked by frequency of contributor requests and potential impact on satisfaction and retention.

🗓️
Calendar Sync

Allow users to sync period and ovulation predictions directly to Google, Outlook, or Apple Calendar — the single most-requested feature across the entire study.

✏️
Editable Period Dates

Give users the ability to adjust predicted dates and enter variable cycle lengths, especially critical for users with irregular periods who currently find the tool inaccurate.

📊
Multi-Month View

Extend the calendar output to display upcoming period and ovulation days for at least 3 future months, enabling users to plan ahead with confidence.

🩺
Symptom Tracker

Introduce logging for common period symptoms — mood, flow intensity, pain levels — to make the tool competitive with dedicated tracking apps and more clinically useful.

📧
Email Preference Centre

Separate reminder emails from content/resource emails with individual frequency controls. Contributors want autonomy over what they receive and how often.

📱
Mobile App Version

Build a PWA or native app to serve the 58% of contributors who primarily use mobile apps for period tracking — reducing friction and enabling push notifications over email.

🌡️
Ovulation Date Estimation

Provide an estimated ovulation date alongside period predictions, supporting contributors who use the tool for both health awareness and family planning.

🧬
Personalised Predictions

Collect optional lifestyle data — stress, diet, exercise, sleep — to improve prediction accuracy over time and generate personalised insights akin to leading period apps.
